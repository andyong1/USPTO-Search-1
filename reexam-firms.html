Law firm scorecard — ex parte reexamination

Which firm appeared for each side of a reexamination, and how the request was decided. Both sides are read from the correspondence blocks on the determination's cover sheet — the bare 7590 block names the patent owner's correspondent, and a separately labeled block names the third-party requester's counsel. That is the same document recording the outcome, so firm and result come from one source.

Firms are shown as of the determination, not today. Powers of attorney change hands, so a firm listed here may no longer represent the patent owner.

Coverage & how to read the rates

The grant rate is a weak discriminator. Because the Office orders reexamination on the large majority of requests, almost every firm with any volume lands near the corpus average, and a small firm going undefeated is the expected outcome rather than evidence of skill. Tested formally against the corpus rate, the great majority of these records are not statistically distinguishable from average — so read the differences between firms as noise unless the volume is large and the gap is wide. Where a firm does separate from the baseline it is usually a reflection of the kind of case it takes rather than of quality.

Filer type (firm / individual / other) is a heuristic: the cover sheet does not say what kind of correspondent it names. It exists to let you exclude self-represented parties and filing services from a firm leaderboard, not as an authoritative classification.

Where OCR interleaved the cover sheet's two columns the firm name is simply absent from the text; those proceedings are reported as unattributed rather than assigned a firm invented from layout noise. A patent-owner-requested reexamination legitimately has no requester block at all.
